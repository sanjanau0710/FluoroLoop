The science, illustrated

Removal is not the same as destruction.

Conventional treatment can separate PFAS from water. FluoroLoop explores what happens next: treating the residual stream and accounting for fluorine.

Figure 01 · Conceptual treatment trainIllustration, not a facility design
EPA identifies granular activated carbon, anion exchange, reverse osmosis, and nanofiltration as effective PFAS removal approaches. Separation does not, by itself, destroy the captured PFAS. EPA treatment fact sheet ↗
Capture

Move PFAS out of water

Adsorbents and membranes can produce cleaner water, but leave spent media or a concentrate requiring responsible handling.

Evidence: EPA drinking-water treatment guidance
Destroy

Challenge C–F bonds

Emerging destruction approaches use electrochemical, thermal, plasma, or reductive conditions. Outcomes depend strongly on feed chemistry and operating conditions.

Evidence: ITRC PFAS treatment guidance
Verify

Follow the fluorine

Loss of a parent PFAS is not proof of complete mineralization. Track inorganic fluoride, residual organofluorine, and any unaccounted fraction.

Evidence: Smith et al., Environmental Science & Technology (2024)

What should scientists actually measure?

Check parent PFAS and transformation products, measure released inorganic fluoride, and assess total/extractable organofluorine where appropriate. A credible fluorine mass balance must also report analytical recovery and uncertainty.

Important: the percentages in the Fluorine Passport are a demonstration of how results could be displayed. They are not predictions of real fluoride recovery.

Model boundaries

What FluoroLoop will and will not claim

✓ Decision supportCompare technologies and make tradeoffs visible.
✓ Transparent modelingSeparate source-backed data, derived values, and assumptions.
✓ UncertaintyKeep unknown fluorine and confidence ranges visible.
✕ No regulatory adviceNot a substitute for professional engineering or permitting decisions.
✕ No fake PFAS sensorThe Field Node monitors process variables, not magical direct PFAS detection.
✕ AI is not the calculatorThe deterministic engine chooses; AI explains grounded results.

FluoroLoop in 30 seconds

Problem: PFAS treatment is often discussed as “removal” or “destruction,” but that does not automatically explain where the fluorine ends up.

What FluoroLoop does: It compares treatment pathways, ranks them based on user priorities, then generates a Fluorine Passport showing recovery, inorganic fluoride, residual organofluorine, and uncertainty.

Why it is different: It combines treatment comparison with fluorine fate and circularity instead of stopping at a single removal percentage. All numbers are illustrative; the AI and hardware sections are simulations, not connected systems.
